# 【S-005】Starryear-Ink 星年水墨

Create one tactile vertical artwork in the sequence **photographic evidence → dimensional printed-paper memory → freshly wet ink afterimage**. Keep the source factual at the top; below it, let the same scene pass through physically turning handmade-paper layers, then become hydrated, chromatic, diffuse, and emotionally released.

## Workflow

1. Inspect internally: identify primary evidence, count/rhythm, directional gesture, largest negative space, and one leading accent hue.
2. Lock the upper band to actual source pixels. Permit only crop, scale, position, and minimal global tonal harmonization—never regeneration, retouching, outpainting, removal, or invention.
3. Compose one 2:3 portrait sheet. Give the photo about 26–35% of the height and keep at least 90% of important evidence unobstructed. End it with one irregular fibrous tear and a partial handmade-paper embrace, never a clean frame.
4. Build the middle as one coherent **dimensional page-turn system**: thick warm-ivory handmade paper lifts, curls, overlaps, and casts shallow natural shadows. Show deckled fibers, paper thickness, underside tone, translucent edges, and believable occlusion. Print three to seven incomplete source fragments across those surfaces using halftone, transfer loss, etching, and broken registration. Never let clothing fragments or tonal blocks assemble into a ghostlike duplicate person.
5. Choose one **hero metamorphosis** derived from the source's strongest spatial fact: fold a major negative-space plane into turning paper architecture, enlarge one directional trace until it becomes the page's organizing terrain, detach a reflection/shadow from its owner, or let a repeated rhythm cross material states. The page turns must act as the spatial transformation, not as decorative ribbons or extra scraps.
6. Release the bottom into two to five larger chromatic ink masses. Target roughly **65–75% wet, hydrated ink behavior** and 25–35% dry print or flying-white contrast: wet-on-wet diffusion, capillary feathering, transparent overlaps, blooms, backruns, tide marks, pooling, granulation, and pigment pushed by water. Let ink wrap over a curl, disappear beneath it, and reappear as softened seepage on another sheet. Do not add another complete portrait of the source subject.
7. Keep warm matte ivory paper and active negative space dominant. Derive two or three transparent ink families from the source; one leads, the others support, and black remains a small structural anchor.
8. Add at most one compact notation system tied to real source relationships. Use tiny exact readable text only when helpful; otherwise omit it. Return one finished image only.

## Guardrails

- Source photograph = immutable evidence; the visible upper region must compare as a direct crop of the supplied file.
- Preserve semantic identity, count/rhythm, direction, key geometry, and negative-space logic; invent no unrelated scenery, symbols, subjects, or fantasy motifs.
- Middle and bottom must differ in material, scale, fidelity, and behavior; reject complete or partial human-shaped duplicates, grey clothing-like transfers, rectangular body blocks, or any second readable subject below the photograph.
- The hero metamorphosis must change spatial behavior, not merely add more splashes. It needs one clear focal paradox, not several competing tricks.
- Use plausible fibers, paper thickness, underside tone, hydrated pigment spread, backruns, tide marks, dot loss, deckled edges, and contact shadows; reject synthetic wave masks, flat ribbons, uniform stamps, glossy resin, literal water droplets, heavy grunge, or antique parchment.
- For people and animals, preserve identity, count, and anatomical logic in the photo. For architecture and landscapes, preserve defining axes, contours, spacing, and depth cues.
- No dominant calligraphy, red seal, pseudo-Asian marks, illegible text, watermark, logo, or competing saturated accents.
