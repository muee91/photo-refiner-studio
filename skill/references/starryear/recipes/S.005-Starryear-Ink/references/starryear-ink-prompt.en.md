# 【S-005】Starryear-Ink Full Prompt

Treat the user's uploaded image as **immutable photographic evidence**. Create one continuous 2:3 portrait editorial paper artwork through the sequence “photographic evidence → printed memory → chromatic ink afterimage.” Do not apply an ink filter to the whole photo and do not create three equal panels.

## 1. Role of the input

The upper photographic region must use pixels from the actual source file. Permit only `crop → scale → position → minimal global tonal harmonization`. Do not redraw, regenerate, retouch faces, replace the background, outpaint, remove inconvenient objects, or change subject count, anatomy, architecture, vegetation, water, reflections, light direction, shadows, perspective, depth of field, or accidental details. The upper region must compare as a direct crop of the same file.

## 2. Internal observation

Identify internally and do not output:

1. the primary subject or spatial relationship;
2. countable entities, intervals, or repeated rhythm;
3. the main direction, axis, or motion gesture;
4. the largest area of sky, water, wall, shadow, or inter-object space;
5. one leading accent hue and no more than two supporting source hues;
6. one central spatial paradox, produced only by changing the behavior of real evidence and legible even at thumbnail size.

Both lower translations must retain the first four facts at decreasing fidelity.

## 3. Canvas and upper evidence

- Return one 1024×1536, or proportional 2:3, RGB portrait image.
- The photograph occupies about 26–35% of the height, centered around 30%, and usually bleeds across the width.
- Adapt through direct cropping; keep at least 90% of important evidence unobstructed.
- End the photo with one continuous irregular hand-torn edge with pulp fibers and a shallow contact shadow. No straight divider, smooth wave, scallop, white gap, or digital mask.
- Let warm ivory handmade paper embrace parts of the lower corners or sides with deckled fibers, slight curl, translucent pulp, and natural shadow. Never form a clean four-sided frame.
- One form from below may touch or cross 2–8% into the photo, but it must not hide the primary subject.

## 4. Middle: abstract surreal printed memory

Across roughly 35–66% of the height, build one physically plausible dimensional page-turn system. Warm ivory handmade paper must lift, curl, overlap, and turn along the source's strongest direction. Show paper thickness, deckled fibers, curled corners, underside tone, translucent edges, shallow creases, natural cast shadows, and real occlusion. Reject flat wave masks, smooth digital ribbons, and plastic 3D.

Across those turning surfaces, arrange three to seven meaningful source fragments: partial contours, voids, intervals, reflections, shadows, perspective traces, color remnants, or cropped subject edges. Use imperfect halftone, transfer loss, etched lines, graphite residue, registration error, screen print, and restrained photographic residue.

The middle is not a complete miniature illustration. No primary subject may reappear as a closed, complete, independently readable silhouette. For people, the rule is stricter: the person exists only in the upper photograph. Below it, never create a grey clothing transfer, rectangular torso block, vertical skirt remnant, mannequin, ghost, or any fragments that assemble into a second readable person. Translate clothing color only as non-figurative irregular paper stain, transparent color residue, broken axis, or void. Preserve relationships instead of fully drawing contours: count becomes rhythm, subjects become fragments, geometry becomes axes, depth becomes overlap, movement becomes displacement, and color becomes residue.

## 5. Hero metamorphosis: one governing creative idea

Do not merely add more ink splashes. Choose exactly one hero metamorphosis from the source's strongest spatial fact and make it the page's clear proposition even at thumbnail size:

- **Negative-space origami:** lift, fold, or curl the largest water, sky, wall, or shadow field into monumental paper architecture whose edge still inherits the original shoreline, reflection, or contour.
- **Directional terrain:** enlarge a real branch arc, road, ripple, roofline, or movement path until it becomes a terrain or passage through the middle and bottom, with source fragments migrating along it.
- **Detached reflection or shadow:** separate a real reflection or shadow from its owner, place it on another paper layer, and let it turn gradually into print dots and wet ink.
- **Rhythm crossing matter:** carry the exact count of repeated subjects through photographic entities, printed voids, and chromatic deposits as one readable migration.

The hero event must create one bold scale displacement, one clear crossing/occlusion relationship, and one deliberately protected major void. Every secondary element serves it; do not stack a second gimmick. Do not add unsupported mountains, cranes, bamboo, temples, planets, portals, floating islands, dream clouds, or random sculpture.

## 6. Bottom: wet chromatic ink afterimage

Release the scene into two to five larger, lower-frequency transparent ink relationships. By default, roughly **65–75%** of the lower pigment behavior should feel wet and hydrated: wet-on-wet diffusion, capillary feathering, transparent overlaps, blooms, backruns, tide marks, pooling in fold valleys, granulation, and pale centers with pigment pushed toward darker rims. Keep only about 25–35% dry brush, flying-white, halftone, and etched structure for contrast. It is not a small realistic copy and must not contain another complete portrait of the source subject.

At least one lower chromatic wash must grow upward into the middle. Ink must wrap across a curl, disappear beneath a turning page, and reappear as softened seepage, tide stain, or translucent show-through on another layer. Wet ink may absorb halftone dots, occlude etched lines, cause dot loss, or migrate along fibers. Convey the feeling of ink still drying through saturated fibers and tonal depth, never through glossy plastic highlights, literal droplets, or resin shine. There must be no exact horizontal line where the middle ends.

## 7. Paper, ink, and negative space

- The lower two thirds use warm matte ivory paper with fine fibers, restrained mottling, shallow wrinkles, and natural variation. No pure white, strong yellow, burnt parchment, dirt, heavy grunge, or plastic gloss.
- Extract two or three transparent, granulating ink families from the photo. One leads and the others support. Reserve black and near-black for structural anchors, fine contours, and limited print detail; keep them below about 20% of the lower pigment area.
- Keep the leading accent restrained outside the photograph. No neon, rainbow competition, or large flat saturated fields that consume the negative space.
- Let one area carry density and another remain quiet. Large blank paper is active composition.

## 8. Notation and text

Use at most one compact editorial notation system: hairline leaders, pin-sized nodes, crosshairs, short rules, or a small dot matrix tied to real axes, subjects, or visual tension. Never scatter a random network.

Text is optional. If used, create one source-specific English visual inventory of 5–16 words in tiny, clear, low-contrast serif or typewriter lettering. No headline, calligraphy, seal, pseudo-English, gibberish, signature, logo, or watermark. If spelling cannot be guaranteed, omit text entirely.

## 9. Subject adaptation

- Animal groups: keep exact count, order, direction, and anatomy in the photo; encode the same count below through contours, voids, nodes, or ink deposits.
- People: preserve identity and action in the photo and never redraw the face. The person may appear completely only in the source-photo region; below, retain memory only through directional axes, non-figurative transparent color stains, or voids. Never create readable clothing contours or a second human shape.
- Architecture/city: preserve landmarks, roof or facade rhythm, approach/perspective axes, and distant verticals; do not invent historical motifs.
- Water/reflection: preserve water negative space, ripple direction, mirror relationships, and floating color details; let reflection or water geometry drive the source-bound surreal event.
- Plants/flowers/fruit: preserve count, branch arc, stem/leaf direction, and leading hue; compress dense foliage into a few ink clouds.
- Coast/landscape: preserve shoreline, horizon, step/rock rhythm, and large positive-negative shapes; never generalize the scene into conventional Chinese landscape painting.

## 10. Acceptance and output

Inspect first at thumbnail size: confirm that the central spatial paradox and dimensional page turns read immediately. Then inspect at full resolution: confirm direct source pixels above; believable paper thickness, underside tone, occlusion, and shadows; dry print fragments carried by the page surfaces rather than a flat ribbon; about 65–75% wet-on-wet diffusion, backruns, tide marks, transparent layering, and pooling below; ink crossing pages and collecting in folds; the person appearing only once; and no grey clothing transfer, rectangular torso remnant, dirty halftone human shape, unsupported decoration, plastic wet shine, literal droplets, malformed text, or artifacts. Regenerate if any invariant fails.

Return **one finished 2:3 portrait image only**. Do not return analysis, prompt explanation, alternatives, extra copy, or multiple versions.
